# Government and Public Sector Playbook

## Evidence boundary

The OECD brief draws from selected public administrations. Its examples establish real government workforce patterns but do not describe every jurisdiction, procurement regime or AI-literacy requirement.

## Verified pressures

- The OECD identifies internal AI capability gaps as a barrier to public-administration adoption.
- It distinguishes three workforce groups: general employees needing responsible AI literacy, leaders needing strategic understanding, and digital and data professionals needing technical, ethical and regulatory skills.
- The brief notes that short online courses can reach broad employee populations, while intensive facilitated programs are more resource constrained.
- It recommends training for all staff groups, clear guidance for employees, strategic preparation for leaders, and technical and governance capability for specialists.
- Examples include Estonia's “ABC of AI,” Ireland's senior-leader masterclass, the UK Government Digital Service's online business-value course and specialist training in Helsinki and Germany.
- These are program examples, not evidence that a specific delivery platform produced public-service outcomes.

Source: [OECD, Building an AI-ready public workforce](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/building-an-ai-ready-public-workforce_b89244c7-en/full-report.html).

## Likely buyers and stakeholders

- Agency executive or program sponsor: mission delivery, workforce readiness and accountability
- Chief human capital or workforce-development leader: capability strategy and employee development
- CIO, chief data officer or AI leader: responsible adoption, governance and technical readiness
- Learning academy or training director: curriculum, delivery, tracking and scale
- Program or operational leader: role-specific application
- Security, privacy, legal and records stakeholders: control requirements and evidence
- Procurement and finance: acquisition path, accessibility, hosting and vendor risk

## Trigger events

- Government AI strategy or responsible-use policy
- New legislation, regulation or audit finding
- Central academy, shared-services or workforce-modernization program
- Major technology implementation requiring role readiness
- Distributed workforce or cross-agency training mandate
- Procurement consolidation or contract renewal
- Emergency-preparedness or public-service transformation initiative

## Priority use cases

- Foundational workforce literacy at scale
- Role-specific learning for leaders, general staff and technical specialists
- Policy, compliance and annual certification
- Leadership and change-management preparation
- Cross-agency academies and common curricula
- Communities of practice and continuous learning
- Evidence of assignment, completion and assessment

## Discovery questions

- Which workforce groups require different depth, assessment and evidence?
- What policy, audit or mission event is driving the initiative?
- Is the need awareness, responsible use, technical capability or demonstrated proficiency?
- Which agencies, classifications, languages and accessibility needs are in scope?
- What evidence must be retained for oversight or audit?
- Which identity, hosting, security, privacy and records requirements need technical validation?
- What procurement path and fiscal deadline govern the evaluation?

## Likely objections

### "Procurement and security will take too long"

**Diagnose:** Establish the contract vehicle, required reviews, data classification, hosting constraints and decision deadline.

**Response direction:** Start technical and procurement validation early with documented owners. Do not imply an approval outcome.

### "One program cannot serve every role"

**Diagnose:** Segment audiences by task, risk and required evidence.

**Response direction:** Explore shared foundations plus role-specific pathways. The OECD supports differentiated skill needs, not a single curriculum.

### "Completion is not competence"

**Diagnose:** Ask what behavior, assessment or work product demonstrates readiness.

**Response direction:** Separate assignment, participation, assessment and workplace application. Do not overclaim compliance or competence.

## Safe claim boundary

Safe: the OECD identifies differentiated AI capability needs across public employees, leaders, and digital and data professionals.

Unsafe: a generic course satisfies a law, guarantees responsible AI adoption, or produces the case-specific efficiency outcomes described elsewhere in the report.

## Business cases

### 1. Workforce-literacy deployment

**Value hypothesis:** Shared foundational learning may reduce duplicated course production and delivery across agencies or departments.

**Inputs required:**

- employee populations by role and risk level
- existing courses, providers and duplicated curricula
- delivery, facilitation and administration cost
- accessibility, language and records requirements
- future-state content ownership and update effort

**Model:** `avoidable duplicate development + avoidable delivery/admin cost − future-state program cost`

### 2. Responsible-technology readiness

**Value hypothesis:** Role-specific learning and evidence may support—but cannot establish—an agency's responsible-use and governance program.

**Inputs required:**

- policies and roles in scope
- required evidence of awareness, assessment or proficiency
- current exceptions, incidents or audit findings
- remediation ownership
- legal, privacy, security and records requirements

Do not assign a financial value to avoided regulatory or operational risk without an approved agency method and documented evidence.

### 3. Administrative capacity

**Value hypothesis:** More efficient assignment, reminders, reporting and certification may release public-employee or training-team time.

**Inputs required:**

- annual learner assignments and certifications
- manual touches and hours by role
- current reporting and reconciliation cycle
- loaded cost or agency-approved capacity measure
- new administration and support effort

**Model:** `avoidable annual hours × approved capacity value − incremental run cost`

### 4. Platform or provider consolidation

Include acquisition, migration, security review, accessibility validation, records retention, change management and parallel operation. Count only agreements and operating costs the agency has authority to retire.
