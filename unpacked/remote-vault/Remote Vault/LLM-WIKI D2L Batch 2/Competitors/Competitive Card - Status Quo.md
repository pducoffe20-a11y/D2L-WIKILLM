# Competitive Card - Status Quo

## Governance

- **Current approved guidance:** [[Discovery and Qualification Playbook]], [[Migration and Transition Qualification]] and [[Change Management Consulting Positioning]] support current-state, scope, acceptance and change-readiness discovery.
- **Historical intelligence:** generic “status quo is not an option” language appears in older pitch material. It is not evidence that a specific buyer must change.
- **Automated competitive signal:** none. Status quo is the buyer's current operating choice, not a vendor page.
- **Boundary:** cost of inaction, urgency and compelling event remain hypotheses until the buyer supplies evidence.

## Where they fit

Staying put is rational when the current environment meets priority needs, the impact of gaps is low or unmeasured, stakeholders are not aligned, or migration and change costs exceed the expected value.

## Likely buyer rationale

- Familiarity and continuity matter more than incremental improvement.
- Teams have already invested in integrations, content, training and governance.
- Change fatigue, capacity or risk is high.
- There is no funded priority, executive sponsor or compelling event.
- The buyer sees gaps but has not quantified their operational or business impact.

## Discovery questions

- What is working well enough that it must be protected?
- Which limitations create measurable learner, staff, risk or growth impact?
- What happens if nothing changes over the next 12–24 months?
- Which event, deadline or strategic initiative changes the decision?
- Who experiences the current cost, who owns the outcome and who would fund change?
- What content, data, integrations and workflows make transition risky?
- What minimum evidence would make action safer than delay?

## Safe differentiation

- Make the buyer's current state the comparison baseline.
- Quantify impact with buyer-owned data; keep value as a hypothesis until validated.
- Separate content, data, integrations, authentication, reporting and adoption when discussing migration.
- Offer a bounded next step—workflow validation, technical scoping, proof of concept or business-case workshop—when the buyer lacks enough evidence to decide.
- Acknowledge when staying put is presently the better decision.

## Claims to avoid

- “Doing nothing is not an option.”
- The current platform is obsolete, risky or expensive without buyer evidence.
- D2L will guarantee savings, adoption, outcomes or a disruption-free migration.
- Sunk cost alone makes the current decision irrational.
- An artificial deadline, unsupported ROI or generic fear of falling behind.

## Desired next step

Build a jointly owned current-state baseline with one quantified impact, one decision owner, one change constraint and one bounded validation action.
