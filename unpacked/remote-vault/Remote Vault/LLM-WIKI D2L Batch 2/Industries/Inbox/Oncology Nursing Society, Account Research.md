## Step 3: Public Research – Oncology Nursing Society Profile

### Organization Overview

**Name:** Oncology Nursing Society (ONS)  
**Founded:** 1975  
**Headquarters:** Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania  
**Mission:** Advance oncology nursing science and evidence-based practice; support professional development and patient-centered care.

### Membership & Scale

- **Members:** Approximately 40,000 oncology nurses (RNs, APNs, specialists)
- **Chapters:** 50+ local and regional chapters across North America
- **Membership Types:** Individual RN members, APNs, institutional affiliates, students
- **Demographics:** Oncology nurses across hospitals, outpatient clinics, cancer centers, rural and urban settings

### Education & Certification Programs

#### Continuing Education (CE)
- **ONCC Certification:** Oncology Certified Nurse (OCN) and Advanced Oncology Certified Nurse (AOCN) credentials
- **Annual CE:** Required for credential maintenance and licensure in most states
- **Program Delivery:** Mix of in-person (conferences, chapter events) and online CE
- **Format:** Courses, webinars, journal articles, interactive modules
- **Topics:** Cancer protocols, symptom management, patient communication, survivorship care, emerging therapies

#### Key Education Events
- **Annual Congress:** Largest oncology nursing education event (~3,500+ attendees)
- **Chapter Workshops:** Regional in-person and hybrid education
- **Online Learning:** Growing catalog of on-demand CE courses and certifications

### Revenue Model

- **Dues:** Member dues (~$200-$400/year, estimate)
- **Non-Dues Revenue (Education):** CE programs, certification exam fees, course licensing, conference registration
- **Partnerships:** Pharma partners, cancer centers, medical device companies for sponsored education
- **Institutional Memberships:** Hospitals and cancer centers enrolling staff in member programs

### Strategic Priorities (Inferred from Industry Context)

1. **Credential Currency & Compliance:** Ensure oncology nurses maintain required CE credits; track completion and audit compliance
2. **Member Engagement:** Expand online education to reach geographically dispersed members; reduce barriers to CE access
3. **Non-Dues Revenue Growth:** Grow revenue from CE, certification, and advanced programs (strategic priority for associations)
4. **Content Scale:** Accelerate development of new CE programs to keep pace with evolving cancer treatment (new drugs, protocols, trials)
5. **Quality & Rigor:** Maintain clinical accuracy, peer review, and evidence-based content standards
6. **Digital Accessibility:** Reach members in rural areas, shift workers, and international nurses; support mobile/asynchronous learning

### Challenges (Industry Context)

- **Content Velocity:** Oncology treatment landscape evolves rapidly (new immunotherapies, targeted drugs, clinical trials); CE content must stay current
- **Nurse Burnout & Engagement:** Competing demands for nurses' time; making CE accessible and efficient is member retention lever
- **Compliance & Audit Risk:** Credential bodies require auditable evidence of CE completion; regulatory pressure on accuracy
- **Infrastructure Costs:** Managing CE delivery across 50+ chapters, multiple platforms, and integration with member databases (AMS) is complex
- **Competitive Pressure:** Other nursing associations, medical societies, and corporate training platforms offering competing CE
- **Post-Pandemic Shift:** Transition from in-person to hybrid/online education; member expectations for digital-first access

### Current Platform & Technology (Estimated)

- **AMS/Member Database:** Likely professional association platform (e.g., Wild Apricot, Community Brands, or proprietary)
- **Learning:** Mix of third-party LMS (e.g., Canvas, Moodle, Docebo) + custom/legacy systems
- **E-Commerce:** Course storefront, payment processing, member pricing
- **Integration Gaps:** Limited real-time sync between AMS (member records), learning platform (enrollment, completion), and financial systems
