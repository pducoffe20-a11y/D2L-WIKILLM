# Competitive Card - Canvas

## Governance

- **Current approved guidance:** [Brightspace vs Canvas One Pager](https://d2lmail.sharepoint.com/sites/ProductMarketingAssets/Education/Brightspace%20vs%20Canvas%20One%20Pager.pdf?web=1), updated 2026-05-19. It emphasizes learning design, accessibility strategy, connected capabilities, AI and partnership.
- **Historical intelligence:** older comparison matrices and differentiator documents remain discoverable in Product Marketing and in the historical CI inventory. Their feature-level claims are not current guidance.
- **Automated competitive signal:** [Canvas's official product page](https://www.instructure.com/canvas), captured with Crawlberg on 2026-07-30, positions Canvas around teaching and learning, simple educator workflows, administration, data, mobile apps, accessibility, integrations and tiered offerings.
- **Contradiction to manage:** the approved D2L one-pager describes broader Canvas capabilities as dependent on add-ons, while the current Canvas page promotes expanded AI, analytics and platform tiers. Validate the buyer's proposed tier and included workflows before making a scope comparison.

## Where they fit

Canvas is a credible education LMS option where the buyer values familiar teaching workflows, mobile experiences, a broad integration ecosystem and adjacent Instructure offerings. This is a fit hypothesis based on its current self-positioning, not an endorsement or verified account condition.

## Likely buyer rationale

- Faculty or administrators already know the platform.
- The institution values perceived simplicity and a large education ecosystem.
- Existing integrations, training and course assets make change feel costly.
- A broader Instructure relationship or tier appears to cover the evaluation.

Treat each as a hypothesis until the buyer confirms it.

## Discovery questions

- Which teaching, assessment and feedback workflows matter most, and how are they measured?
- Which capabilities are native to the proposed Canvas tier, and which require separate products or integrations?
- Where do faculty or administrators leave the LMS to complete work?
- How are accessibility remediation, analytics, credentialing and content creation governed today?
- What must be preserved in migration: course structure, question banks, integrations, data history or faculty habits?
- What would make a change worthwhile despite familiarity and sunk cost?

## Safe differentiation

- Evaluate the complete teaching-and-learning workflow, not only the core course interface.
- Show Brightspace against the buyer's required learning-design, automation, analytics, accessibility and content-authoring scenarios.
- Make services, migration validation and adoption support part of the decision criteria when they matter.
- Confirm packaging live. Demonstrate a D2L capability; do not infer that Canvas lacks it.

## Claims to avoid

- Canvas is only a basic LMS, is not innovative or cannot support advanced learning.
- A named capability is absent, limited or an add-on without checking the current proposed tier.
- D2L will reduce cost, improve outcomes or simplify the ecosystem without a buyer-specific baseline.
- Canvas market-share, uptime, partner-count or AI claims copied from its website as independent facts.
- Universal claims about faculty preference, migration effort or implementation speed.

## Desired next step

Agree on two or three scored workflows and the exact product scope each vendor must demonstrate, then identify migration objects requiring technical validation.
