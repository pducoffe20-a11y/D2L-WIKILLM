# Proof Card - CHIME - Accelerated Digital Learning Launch

## Context

The College of Healthcare Information Management Executives planned a nine-month digital-learning implementation. When COVID-19 disrupted its face-to-face education, CHIME needed to make useful online learning available rapidly.

## Buyer problem

Respond to a time-critical disruption without waiting for the full future-state platform and content vision.

## What changed

CHIME and D2L narrowed the initial scope, planned an iterative rollout, and combined CHIME’s team with D2L Learning and Creative Services. The initial experience used Brightspace, Intelligent Agents, and a commerce integration.

## Reported outcome

- Initial launch accelerated from a planned nine months to two weeks.
- Online learning became available to members and subscribers while later capabilities and content were staged for subsequent iterations.
- The platform created a path to reach learners unable to attend in-person events.

## Safe claim

CHIME reports that it reduced an initial nine-month implementation plan to a two-week launch by narrowing scope, working iteratively, and augmenting its team with D2L services.

## Do not overstate

- Do not promise a two-week implementation for another customer.
- Do not describe the two-week launch as delivery of the complete nine-month vision.
- Do not omit the crisis context, scope reduction, customer effort, or services contribution.
- Do not use CHIME’s commentary about security discussions as a universal competitive claim.

## Best fit

- Associations and healthcare education
- Buyers with a hard launch date or disrupted delivery model
- Phased implementation and minimum viable launch discussions
- Prospects considering services to augment internal capacity

## Discovery questions

- What must be live by the compelling date, and what can follow later?
- Which content, integrations, and workflows are essential for the first audience?
- Where does the internal team need implementation or instructional-design capacity?
- What decision authority is available to protect a narrow first-release scope?
