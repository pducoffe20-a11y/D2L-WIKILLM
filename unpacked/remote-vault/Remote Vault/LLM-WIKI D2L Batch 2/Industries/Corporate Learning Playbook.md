# Corporate Learning Playbook

## Evidence boundary

The World Economic Forum findings describe surveyed employers and forward-looking expectations. They establish sector pressure, not a specific account's priorities and not a D2L outcome.

## Verified pressures

- Surveyed employers expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030.
- Respondents reported that 50% of their workforce had completed training through learning-and-development initiatives, up from 41% in the 2023 report.
- AI and big data, networks and cybersecurity, and technological literacy were identified as the three fastest-growing skills.
- Skill priorities vary materially by industry. The report therefore supports differentiated pathways more strongly than a universal curriculum.
- Employers cited expected productivity, competitiveness and retention benefits from training, but these are expectations rather than measured returns.

Source: [World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025 — Skills Outlook](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/).

## Likely buyers and stakeholders

These are seller hypotheses to validate:

- CLO or head of learning: portfolio strategy, adoption, skill development and evidence
- CHRO or talent leader: workforce planning, mobility, retention and role readiness
- Business-unit leader: time to proficiency and operational performance
- CIO, CTO or data leader: AI, cybersecurity, integrations, identity and governance
- Learning operations or LMS administrator: scale, automation, reporting and administration
- Finance or procurement: consolidation, cost-to-serve and measurable value

## Trigger events

- AI or automation strategy moving from experimentation to workforce rollout
- New operating model, acquisition or platform-consolidation initiative
- Rapid hiring, new-market entry or repeated onboarding bottlenecks
- Skills taxonomy, academy or internal-mobility program
- Compliance or product-certification expansion
- Leadership request for evidence of learning impact

## Priority use cases

- Role-based onboarding and time to proficiency
- AI, data and cybersecurity literacy
- Upskilling and reskilling pathways
- Product, partner or customer education
- Leadership and manager development
- Platform consolidation and learning-operations automation
- Skills recognition through badges or credentials

Treat each as a hypothesis until the buyer confirms the audience, workflow, baseline and desired business outcome.

## Discovery questions

- Which roles will change most in the next 12–24 months?
- What observable work should people perform differently after learning?
- Where does onboarding or reskilling currently lose time?
- Which learning systems, content sources and data flows are duplicated?
- How are completion, proficiency, application and business impact measured separately?
- Which business leader owns the outcome outside the learning team?
- What would make the initiative important enough to fund this cycle?

## Likely objections

### "We already have an LMS"

**What may be behind it:** The buyer sees the project as platform replacement rather than an operating problem.

**Diagnose:** Ask which audiences, workflows and outcomes the current environment handles poorly.

**Response direction:** Focus on the unresolved business requirement. Do not manufacture a replacement case.

### "Employees do not have time for more training"

**What may be behind it:** Workload pressure, irrelevant content or weak manager support.

**Diagnose:** Ask which learning is mandatory, which can be role-based, and where people repeat content they already know.

**Response direction:** Explore targeted pathways, applied learning and manager-supported time. Do not promise engagement.

### "We cannot prove ROI"

**What may be behind it:** No baseline, unclear ownership or outcomes too distant from learning activity.

**Diagnose:** Establish current volume, labor, cycle time, errors, readiness measures and operational owner.

**Response direction:** Build a bounded value hypothesis and measurement plan. Do not use WEF expectations as a forecast.

## Safe claim boundary

Safe: corporate leaders are planning for substantial skill change, and the WEF survey shows increased attention to ongoing training.

Unsafe: every company needs the same skills, an LMS will close the skills gap, or training will deliver the report's expected productivity and retention benefits.

## Business cases

Build these with buyer-supplied baselines. Keep each lever separate so soft assumptions cannot hide inside a total ROI figure.

### 1. Faster onboarding and role readiness

**Value hypothesis:** Reducing avoidable time between hire and independently productive work may return manager, trainer and employee capacity.

**Inputs required:**

- hires per year by role
- current days to defined readiness
- target reduction supported by a changed process
- learner, manager and trainer hours per cohort
- loaded labor cost or another buyer-approved capacity value

**Model:** `annual hires × validated days reduced × buyer-approved daily capacity value`

Do not equate course completion with proficiency or revenue contribution.

### 2. Learning-operations efficiency

**Value hypothesis:** Consolidation and automation may reduce enrollment, reporting, reconciliation and platform-administration effort.

**Inputs required:**

- annual enrollments and evaluation cycles
- minutes per current manual step
- loaded administrative cost
- systems, licenses, integrations and support work that can actually be retired
- future-state operating effort

**Model:** `avoidable labor + retired run cost − new run cost`

Use [[Proof Card - Year Up United - Consolidation and Administrative Efficiency]] as customer-specific precedent, not the prospect's estimate.

### 3. Skills-program scale

**Value hypothesis:** A common platform and reusable pathways may allow the existing team to serve more learners or business units.

**Inputs required:**

- current learners, programs and completions per learning FTE
- content-development and maintenance hours
- localization and business-unit duplication
- forecast demand
- quality and proficiency measures that must remain stable

**Decision gate:** The case is credible only when the buyer confirms incremental demand, capacity constraints and the work that changes.
