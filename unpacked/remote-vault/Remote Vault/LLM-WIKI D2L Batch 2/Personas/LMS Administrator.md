# LMS Administrator

## Seller truth

The named LMS administrator and manager roles identified in the Compliance Training Product Brief — the day-to-day operators responsible for user management, course configuration, and platform upkeep. This persona is distinct from the Learning Operations Leader, who owns program outcomes rather than daily system operation.

## Why it matters

Persona notes guide discovery without inventing priorities. Administrators are frequently the loudest voice on usability and administrative-burden objections because they carry the workload directly — see the AACSB and PDI proof cards for concrete before/after administrative-time examples this persona will find credible.

## When to use it

Technical demos, day-2 operations conversations, and any discussion of administrative burden, user management, or platform configuration.

## Evidence

The Compliance Training Product Brief (source_url above) names L&D leader, LMS administrator, and manager roles and their needs. This is source-bounded seller guidance — do not assume a Compliance Training-specific need generalizes to every LMS Administrator conversation without checking.

## Seller interpretation

**Interpretation:** This persona is often not the economic buyer, but is frequently a required validator — a deal can stall if this persona doesn't believe day-to-day operations will be easier, not harder.

## Talk track

Walk me through a typical week for you managing the platform today — where does most of your time go?

## Discovery questions

- What takes the most manual effort in your current platform: user management, course setup, reporting, or something else?
- How many hours per week would you estimate go to administrative tasks versus higher-value work?
- What's the biggest source of support tickets or user confusion today?
- Who do you escalate to when something breaks, and how long does resolution typically take?

## Objections and cautions

Titles do not prove authority, budget, or ownership. The Compliance Training Product Brief is scoped to that product line — do not extend its specific claims to unrelated product conversations without checking the source.
