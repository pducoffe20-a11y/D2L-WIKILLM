# Healthcare and Regulated Training Playbook

## Evidence boundary

WHO evidence describes the global health workforce and WHO's own learning model. It does not establish a local provider's staffing conditions, regulatory obligations, accreditation rules or technology need.

## Verified pressures

- WHO reports that the estimated global stock of health workers exceeds 70 million.
- WHO projects a worldwide health-workforce shortage of 11 million by 2030.
- WHO describes workforce shortages as uneven across and within regions and shaped by supply, demand and the ability to employ staff.
- WHO's education-and-training work emphasizes competency-based education, curriculum guidance, accreditation and workforce planning.
- WHO Academy presents a digital-first lifelong-learning model designed for broad, distributed access to health learning.
- Global workforce pressure supports discovery about scalable learning and competency development; it does not prove that online delivery resolves staffing shortages.

Sources: [WHO health workforce education and training](https://www.who.int/teams/health-workforce/health-workforce-education-and-training) and [WHO Academy](https://www.who.int/about/who-academy).

## Likely buyers and stakeholders

- Chief learning, clinical-education or workforce-development leader: program strategy and capacity
- Clinical operations or service-line leader: readiness, scheduling and patient-care context
- Compliance, quality or risk leader: evidence, policy and audit requirements
- Credentialing, certification or continuing-education leader: standards, renewal and records
- CIO, CISO or digital-health leader: security, privacy, identity and integration
- Instructional-design or simulation leader: curriculum, assessment and applied practice
- Procurement, legal and finance: vendor risk, contracting and cost

## Trigger events

- New clinical protocol, policy, technology or regulatory requirement
- Workforce shortage, role expansion or accelerated onboarding
- Compliance finding or audit preparation
- Merger, network standardization or learning-platform consolidation
- Continuing-professional-development or credential expansion
- Need to reach shift-based, remote or distributed staff
- Content-production backlog or high instructor-led delivery cost

## Priority use cases

- Compliance and policy learning with auditable records
- Clinical onboarding and role-based pathways
- Continuing professional development
- Credential, certification and renewal learning
- Blended learning that preserves supervised practice
- Digital-health and AI literacy
- Content updates across facilities and roles
- Communities of practice and nonclinical workforce development

## Proof to use

- [[Proof Card - Epworth HealthCare - Compliance Learning Efficiency]] for customer-specific content speed and cost evidence
- [[Proof Card - CHIME - Accelerated Digital Learning Launch]] for a scoped, services-supported association launch

Neither story proves regulatory compliance, clinical competence or patient outcomes for another organization.

## Discovery questions

- Which requirements demand completion evidence, assessment, observed practice or formal credentialing?
- Which learning must remain supervised or in person?
- How quickly must policy or clinical-content updates reach every affected role?
- What creates the largest scheduling or backfill burden?
- Which workforce groups, facilities, shifts and languages are in scope?
- What learner, patient, employee or credential data would enter the platform?
- Who signs off on clinical accuracy, compliance and competency?

## Likely objections

### "Online learning cannot prove clinical competence"

**Diagnose:** Separate knowledge, decision-making, procedural skill and supervised practice.

**Response direction:** Use digital learning for the appropriate components and preserve observation or simulation where required. Never claim completion equals competence.

### "Security and privacy make integration too risky"

**Diagnose:** Identify data classification, identity model, integrations, retention, hosting and review owners.

**Response direction:** Move to documented technical validation. Do not assume health information must enter the LMS or promise approval.

### "Staff cannot leave patient care for training"

**Diagnose:** Quantify scheduling, backfill, course length, update frequency and which components can be asynchronous.

**Response direction:** Explore role-based and blended delivery while protecting required practice. Do not promise time savings without a baseline.

## Safe claim boundary

Safe: WHO identifies persistent global health-workforce shortages and supports scalable, lifelong, competency-oriented education.

Unsafe: online learning fixes staffing shortages, course completion proves clinical competence, or a platform guarantees compliance, accreditation or patient outcomes.

## Business cases

### 1. Training delivery and backfill

**Value hypothesis:** Moving appropriate knowledge components online may reduce instructor time, travel, room use and clinical backfill while preserving supervised practice.

**Inputs required:**

- learners, sites, shifts and annual training events
- course duration by delivery mode
- instructor, learner and backfill cost
- travel and facility cost
- components that must remain live, observed or simulated

**Model:** `avoidable delivery and backfill cost − new digital delivery, support and supervised-practice cost`

Use [[Proof Card - Epworth HealthCare - Compliance Learning Efficiency]] as customer-specific evidence only.

### 2. Content update speed

**Value hypothesis:** Faster publishing and reuse may reduce the cost and exposure created by slow policy, protocol or course updates.

**Inputs required:**

- annual updates and affected courses
- current elapsed time and labor per update
- outsourced development cost
- review and clinical sign-off time
- sites and roles receiving each change

Measure cycle time and production cost. Do not convert faster publication into avoided clinical risk without validated evidence.

### 3. Onboarding capacity

**Value hypothesis:** Role-based pathways may reduce repeated orientation effort and help a fixed education team support more hires.

**Inputs required:**

- hires by role, site and month
- educator and preceptor hours
- learner backfill or paid training time
- readiness definition and observed-practice requirements
- turnover assumptions approved by workforce leadership

**Model:** `avoidable educator/preceptor hours + avoidable learner time − new program cost`

Do not claim reduced time to clinical competence unless the buyer validates an observed proficiency measure.

### 4. Compliance administration

Quantify assignment, reminder, reconciliation, evidence production and audit-preparation effort. Keep completion, assessment, observed competence, credential status and regulatory compliance as separate measures.
