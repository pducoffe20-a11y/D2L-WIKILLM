## How to Use These Cards

### In a sales conversation (2–5 min)

1. **Listen for the buyer's problem** — admin burden? Slow to launch? Enrollment plateau?
2. **Match to the table above** — find the relevant outcome
3. **Open the proof card** — read the "Buyer Problem" and "Discovery Question" sections first
4. **Ask before telling** — use the discovery question to confirm similarity
5. **State context, then metric** — "Company X, similar to you in [way], achieved [metric]"
6. **Offer the full story** — "Would you like to see how they did it?"

### Building an objection response (5–10 min)

1. Find the relevant proof card in the table above
2. Read the "Buyer Problem" section — does it match your buyer's situation?
3. Read the "Claim Boundary" section in each card — understand what this proof does and doesn't show
4. Open the live D2L customer story (link provided in the card) and verify it hasn't changed
5. Build your response around the discovery question, not the metric

### Preparing a proposal (15–30 min)

1. Identify 2–3 buyer outcomes that matter most
2. Find the corresponding proof cards
3. For each, read the full customer story (via link in the card)
4. Pull 1 specific, contextualized proof point per outcome
5. Always cite: customer name, their baseline, the action they took, the result, and the date

## Proof Card Anatomy

Each card contains these sections:

| Section | What It Is | How to Use It |
|---|---|---|
| **Buyer Problem** | What the customer was struggling with before Brightspace | Test if your buyer has the same problem |
| **Brightspace Solution** | How they used Brightspace (and what else) to solve it | Map to your buyer's potential approach |
| **Measurable Outcome** | Specific, named result with numbers or percentage change | Quote the outcome only after confirming similarity |
| **Discovery Question** | Ask this before you cite the metric | Find out if context actually matches |
| **Source & Date** | Where this came from, when captured | Check the live D2L story before external use |

## Claim Boundaries (Important)

These are verified reports of **customer-specific outcomes**. They are evidence that a result occurred in a named context—not a guarantee, benchmark, average, or forecast for another buyer.

✅ **Safe to say:**  
"Company X, in [context], achieved [metric]. Are you in a similar situation?"

❌ **Never say:**  
"All customers get [metric]" or "You can expect [metric]"

**Before external use:**  
Always open the live D2L customer story link to confirm the metric hasn't changed.
