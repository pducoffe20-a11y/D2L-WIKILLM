## Value classes

Keep these separate in every summary:

| Value class | Meaning | Financial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Incremental contribution | Additional revenue less directly variable costs | May enter cash flow when finance validates demand, price, volume and margin |
| Cashable savings | A budgeted cost will actually be removed or avoided | May enter cash flow with budget-owner confirmation |
| Redeployed capacity | Staff time becomes available for other work | Report hours and equivalent value; do not call it cash savings unless a budget changes |
| Risk-adjusted value | Change in expected loss from a defined risk scenario | Use only with risk-owner estimates of likelihood, impact and residual risk |
| Nonfinancial outcome | Reach, completion, engagement, satisfaction, quality or strategic alignment | Report separately unless the buyer supplies an accepted monetary conversion |

## Core calculations

Use the buyer's preferred finance method.

```text
Net benefit = total quantified benefit - total cost

ROI = net benefit / total cost

Benefit-cost ratio = total quantified benefit / total cost

NPV = Σ [net cash flow in year t / (1 + buyer-approved discount rate)^t]

Payback period = first period in which cumulative net cash flow is at least zero
```

Do not calculate ROI when the denominator is zero, omit material implementation/change costs, or mix capacity value with cash savings.

## Evidence ladder

1. Buyer actuals from finance, operations, HR, institutional research or the learning team.
2. Buyer-approved forecast or strategic plan.
3. Validated pilot or representative workflow sample.
4. D2L customer proof used as an analogous example.
5. External benchmark used only as a range and clearly sourced.
6. Seller assumption, explicitly labeled and awaiting validation.

Customer proof establishes plausibility, not the forecast value. For example:

- [[Proof Card - Year Up United - Consolidation and Administrative Efficiency]] reports 41 administrative hours eliminated per 1,000 enrollments in its context.
- [[Proof Card - Good Roads - Scalable Association Training]] reports tenfold enrollment growth and a self-sustaining program in its context.
- [[Proof Card - CHIME - Accelerated Digital Learning Launch]] reports a two-week scoped launch rather than delivery of the full nine-month vision.
- [[Proof Card - Epworth HealthCare - Compliance Learning Efficiency]] reports cost and production improvements against its prior delivery model.

None of those figures is a default input for another buyer.

## Required controls

- State the baseline period, currency, forecast horizon and owner.
- Separate one-time and recurring costs.
- Include implementation, migration, integration, content, training, change, support and internal labor where material.
- Show conservative, expected and upside cases.
- Use a realization factor for benefits that depend on adoption or process change.
- Identify overlaps between models before adding benefits.
- Name the owner who can validate each material input.
- Reforecast after technical validation, commercial scope and implementation design change.

## External methodology

- [GAO Cost Estimating and Assessment Guide](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-195g) calls for purpose, scope, schedule, technical baseline, work breakdown, ground rules, assumptions, data, sensitivity and risk analysis for reliable estimates.
- [HM Treasury Green Book 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book-appraisal-and-evaluation-in-central-government) frames appraisal around the costs, benefits and risks of alternative ways to achieve objectives.
- [OMB Circular A-94](https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/guidance/circulars/) provides federal benefit-cost and discounting guidance. Use a buyer-approved rate; do not import a government rate into a commercial case automatically.
- [NIST IR 8286A Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8286/a/r1/final) supports scenario-based risk documentation using likelihood and impact in an enterprise risk register.

## Stop conditions

Do not present a quantified result when:

- the baseline is unknown;
- the benefit owner will not validate the inputs;
- value depends on an untested causal link;
- costs are materially incomplete;
- the same benefit appears in two models;
- a customer metric is being used as if it were a forecast benchmark.
